How to read 94.2%

94.2%

Band9AI’s published share of practice estimates, inside the calibration subset it defines, that landed within ±0.5 band of a later official result the user reported. Half a band is the width of one reported step, such as 6.5 beside 7.0.

Read the sentence with the limit still attached. The comparison is against scores users later reported, not against a file of examiner marks that Band9AI holds. It is not a third-party audit. It is not certification. A subset that matches closely can still contain people whose official result sat further away, and the next person to take a mock is not that subset.

A sane order before test day

  1. Take a timed estimate. The free one on band9ai.com is Reading plus Writing Task 2. The $15 Reality Check covers Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking.
  2. Read the limiter, not only the headline band. A 6.5 overall can still hide a Writing criterion or a Listening raw score that will not survive another paper of the same kind.
  3. Train that limiter, then retest it. Stop when the practice band and the kind of miss both move, not when a single essay feels better.
  4. Book the official test when you need a Test Report Form. Nothing on band9ai.com or band9ai.org replaces that form.

Questions

What is a practice band estimate?

It is a band-style number from a timed Band9AI mock, scored against public IELTS criteria. It tells you where that sitting landed and which skill or criterion to train. It is not a Test Report Form and it is not accepted by universities or visa offices.

What does the 94.2% figure mean?

Band9AI publishes 94.2% as the share of practice estimates in a defined calibration subset that fell within ±0.5 band of a later official IELTS result reported by the user. It is not a third-party audit, not examiner certification, and not a promise that your next official score will match.

Will my official score match the estimate?

It might be close, and it might not. The tasks, your condition on the day, and the fact that one number is a practice estimate and the other is an examiner score all sit between them. Use the estimate to choose what to fix, then sit the official test when you are ready.
