Speaking

Speaking is four equal scores

There is no mark out of 40, and there is no Task 2 to outweigh the rest. The examiner scores four criteria. The Speaking band is their average.

Direct answer

IELTS Speaking uses four equally weighted criteria: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. The average of those four is the Speaking band. A single 6 pulls that average down. It does not replace the other three. Unlike Writing, nothing on the public scoring page gives one part of Speaking extra weight.

The four criteria

Select a criterion to read the kind of miss that keeps a practice speaking sample around Band 6. These are study notes. They are not the official descriptor text.

Fluency and coherence is whether the answer keeps going and stays followable. Around Band 6, replies are often understandable and still stop early: a long pause, a restart, or a second sentence that repeats the first. Memorised paragraphs fail here when the question was slightly different from the one you practised. More grammar drills will not lengthen an answer you never finished.

What to train depends on which quarter moved

A “speak faster” plan fixes fluency and leaves pronunciation untouched. A word list fixes neither pauses nor grammar that collapses in a longer sentence. Name the criterion, practise that one under a timer, then speak again. The Speaking band only moves when the average moves.

Questions

How is the IELTS Speaking band calculated?

The examiner scores four criteria with equal weight: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. The Speaking band is their average, reported as a whole or half band. There is no second task and no mark out of 40.

Does my lowest Speaking criterion become my Speaking band?

A 6 on fluency beside 7 for vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation averages 6.75, which rounds to 7. The 6 pulls the average down. It does not replace the other three.

Does pronunciation count as much as grammar?

Yes. IELTS weights the four Speaking criteria equally. Pronunciation is one quarter of the Speaking band, the same share as grammar, vocabulary, or fluency.
